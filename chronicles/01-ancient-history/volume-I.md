## Preface

Whoever seeks the beginnings of our history must first reckon with its losses. Kingdoms have perished whose names we cannot give, and peoples have left works behind them without leaving an account of their making. Even where a name survives, the deeds attached to it may have passed through many hands before reaching ours.

We have undertaken this work so that a reader may find a beginning from which to pursue the history of Eides. We shall relate what can be gathered, distinguish the reckoning of years from the ordering of events, and leave a question open where we cannot answer it. Those who seek the succession of every ancient ruler will find much wanting. We would rather acknowledge that want than supply it with names of our own invention.

The greatest difficulty concerns the ages before Wereth's Last Cleansing. Of these we possess fragments, traditions, and the remains of things whose makers are forgotten. A ruin may establish that a people built upon a certain ground; it cannot by itself tell us the name of their king, the laws under which they lived, or the cause of their departure.

## I. Concerning the Reckoning of Ancient Years

The years in this work are reckoned from the beginning of the Bulwarks, including those ages in which no such reckoning was known. The reader should therefore understand an ancient date as a calculation made afterward. Where the calculation is uncertain, we shall say so.

It is often possible to place one event before another without knowing the interval between them. Such an order is useful and should be preserved. It does not entitle us to assign a year to either event.

## Foundation: Of the World, the Gods, and the Weave

### Eides and the Planes of Existence

Eides is the material world in which the kingdoms and peoples of this history have their homes. It is one of the worlds called **planes of existence**, also known as dimensions. The two terms describe the same thing. Beyond Eides lie other planes, each with its own nature, inhabitants, and forms of magical energy.

The seven core planes are Eides, Infernus, Anoxis, Bargol, Eterfröst, Antikythera, and Glunoth. All lie within the surrounding **Astral Plane**, which contains both these worlds and the Magic Weave. The Weave permeates and surrounds everything within the Astral Plane, flowing through the other planes and the space between them.

The seven core planes are only part of what exists within the Astral Plane. Other planes of varying sizes are known to exist there, alongside creatures native to the Astral Plane itself and others originating elsewhere. Much of this surrounding realm remains unexplored, and knowledge of its extent, inhabitants, and nature is still fragmentary.

**Eides** is associated with arcane energy. Its diversity of lands, peoples, and living creatures forms the setting of these Chronicles.

**Infernus** is a plane of fire, demons, and suffering, associated with infernal energy.

**Anoxis** is a plane of corrupted and mutated creatures, associated with blood energy.

**Bargol** is a dark plane inhabited by shadow creatures. Its characteristic energy is dark chaos energy.

**Eterfröst** is a world of snow, frost, and silence, associated with splinter energy.

**Antikythera** is a mechanical plane of clockworks, gears, and ticking mechanisms. Its characteristic energy is flux energy.

**Glunoth** is a monumental plane of ancient runes, golems, and obelisks, associated with rune energy.

The energy associated with each plane is not confined to that world. These forms of energy can manifest elsewhere and serve different magical purposes.

### The Weave of Magic

The Magic Weave is the principal source of magic. It permeates the planes and surrounds them within the Astral Plane. Arcane, infernal, blood, dark chaos, splinter, flux, and rune energy are the seven forms associated with the core planes.

The planes are anchored in the surrounding Weave. Bringing them into contact requires immense energy. When two or more approach closely enough, a connection can form through which creatures, matter, and energy pass from one world into another. Such an event is called a dimensional convergence, a meeting of planes.

These connections are often brief. The exchange can hold the worlds together for a time, until the force of the Weave pulls them apart and returns them toward their anchor points. A creature found in Eides may therefore have originated in another plane, and its presence alone does not establish how or when it arrived.

### The Pantheon of Gods

The divine royal house is headed by **Niir**, King of the Gods, whose domains are light, life, and victory. The association between light and divinity also survives in the Old Language, in which *Niir* signifies light and, symbolically, God.

Three divine dukes hold named domains: **Phames**, health and medicine; **Summus**, balance; and **Solus**, faith. The royal house also includes **Ados**, a divine prince, and the god **Dhiion**.

**Ceses**, the God of Twilight, is counted among the outcast gods. His domains are night, black magic, and gloom. He is also named as the teacher of Solus.

The legends of Summus describe his creation of Divinus and Foedus, the champions of light and darkness, both charged with preserving the balance of the world. Their account appears later in this volume.

Beings of godlike power also appear outside the divine royal house. Among them is **Apophis**, the World Eater, depicted as an immense worm and named as the destroyer of all existence. That title expresses the character attributed to the being; it supplies no date for a destruction of the world.

### Origins and Nature

The Viators were an ancient people of Eides whose existence is now known to very few. Fragments of their history persist among isolated scholars and in hidden libraries. Their name has disappeared from the knowledge of most peoples, although the consequences of their works remain.

Where the Viators came from is unknown. Their presence belongs to the earliest history of Eides, but neither their birthplace nor the manner of their arrival has been established.

They possessed extraordinary wisdom and a command of magic capable of shaping entire continents to their will. Despite this power, they were peaceful and considerate. Their care extended to everything around them, from a squirrel to the mountains themselves. The smallest creature received the same dedication as the greatest works of the world.

Through their understanding of the Weave they built a civilization of great wealth and knowledge. Their magic required an intricate understanding of the nature of things, of their relationships to their surroundings, and of the Weave itself. Every working demanded precision almost inconceivable to a human practitioner. An error at such a level could not be overcome by simply applying greater force.

The Viators were literally weaving the fabric of magic. Their will shaped its structure with an exactness born of understanding, and the world took form through that work.

### The Bright Era and the Teaching of Magic

The Bright Era of Magic is placed at approximately 35000 before the Bulwarks. During this age the Viators taught other peoples of Eides to use the Weave. Their teachings were true and exact, filled with wisdom exceeding what a single human could gather in a lifetime. Learning them required far more than the memorization of words or the repetition of a master's movements. Each act of magic depended upon an understanding of what it changed.

Where a Viator could fashion a realm, even the most gifted mortal pupils could accomplish little beyond the manipulation of objects small enough to fit in a hand. Instruction gave them access to magic without giving them the nature or understanding of their teachers.

The truth of the teaching did not satisfy every pupil. Some saw the powers of their teachers and found their own achievements intolerably small. Knowledge that the Viators considered profound offered little consolation to those who measured their place by the power they could wield.

Over centuries, frustration grew into envy and hatred. Those dissatisfied with their standing in the natural order spread resentment against the Viators and sought more extreme paths to power. The wisdom upon which Viator teaching depended became its greatest weakness: its pupils could desire the results long before they possessed the understanding needed to achieve them.

### Val Shovresk and the New Magic

Val Shovresk eventually discovered that the Weave could be manipulated by force and compelled to serve his will. This gave him access to power far beyond the limits of his former practice. The understanding that had constrained his progress under Viator teaching was no longer required to produce effects of extraordinary magnitude.

What he did not initially understand was the damage his workings inflicted. The same lack of insight that had prevented him from practising Viator magic at their level concealed the consequences of his discovery. Each use injured the Weave irreversibly.

At the beginning of his work, the Weave lay undamaged before him. Its strength allowed feats that later ages would find impossible. The harm accumulated slowly enough that the power immediately available concealed the decline his practice had begun. Over thousands of years, that accumulated damage would drastically diminish the magic that could be cast, making the range and scale of possible workings a dwindling resource.

Shovresk spent years in hiding, refining the technique with others drawn to its sheer power. He eventually became more powerful than most of the Viators themselves. His teachings spread among those who had long resented the limits of their own abilities.

When the Viators learned of his work, they recognized the destruction of something that was not his to destroy. They demanded that the practice cease. By then, its teachings had already passed beyond him and were spreading further with every day.

The Viators also underestimated the power he had acquired. Their own understanding of magic made it difficult to comprehend the full force that such an aggressive and brutal practice could unleash. Their judgment of Shovresk remained bound to limitations he had found a way to overcome, at a cost they understood better than he did.

### Val'kaárun and the Departure

Smaller clashes preceded the final confrontation. Shovresk took part in some, while others involved different practitioners of the spreading magic. The conflict eventually culminated in the Battle of Val'kaárun.

There Shovresk unleashed unimaginable power against the Viators. The forces brought to bear were comparable to direct exposure to the Magic Weave within the Astral Plane itself. Many Viators were killed in the brief and terrible battle.

The survivors abandoned the teaching of the mortal peoples and departed from Eides, bringing their ascendancy to an end.

Their departure is associated with the fall of the ancient civilization, placed at approximately 30000 before the Bulwarks. The date marks the broad historical account. Their destination after leaving Eides remains unknown.

The teaching of magic had spread beyond their control. Shovresk's practice survived them and passed into the civilizations that followed.

### The Three

Before their departure, the Viators created three artifacts of extraordinary power, remembered together as **the Three**. Their scholars concealed them in three separate places and guarded them.

They are described as a cube of impossible form, a hat of changing shape, and a book of alternating magic. Their power exceeded that of mortal magicians. Knowledge of their existence became as rare as knowledge of their makers.

### The Inheritance of the Viators

The Weave continued to suffer under the repeated use of the newer magic. The decline was slow, extending over the civilizations that followed the ancient world. In the age of the Viators, the newer technique could grant ordinary magicians the power to shape continents. In the present age, the creation of a small island would be an extraordinary achievement even for a powerful magician.

Traces of Viator civilization also survived in language. The Old Language, preserved in the names of places and objects, descends from their speech. Words endured in ordinary use after knowledge of the people who first spoke them had faded.

Some scholars attribute the creation of Eides's native species to the Viators. This remains a theory rather than an established account of life's beginnings. Their command of the Weave explains why such powers have been attributed to them, but does not establish every deed assigned to their name.

It is placed at approximately forty-three thousand five hundred years (43.500) before the Bulwarks. The name given to this calamity is the Cleansing of the Gods; it is also called the Apocalypse.

Arderal is said to have brought a great part of Eides under his dominion, subjecting the peoples who lived there. The name of the Cleansing preserves the belief that he had been sent by the gods to scour the world. That men believed this is part of the account. Whether the gods sent him is another question, which the name alone cannot settle.

## VI. Of Wereth's Last Cleansing

The end of the Ancient History period is marked by Wereth's Last Cleansing, that roughly occurred in the year 2700 before the Bulwarks. During the cleansing almost all historic records and vessels of knowledge were destroyed for an unknown reason. 

The name Wereth has survived more securely than an account of the deed. It is not known whether it names a single being, several acting together, or a title. Neither is known a certain account of the means by which the records were destroyed.

The word *Last* has encouraged the belief that earlier cleansings preceded it. 

What survived did so unevenly: fragments of writing, traditions carried in memory, and works that endured after their makers' histories were gone. 

Libraries and archives across Eides lost their contents. Many peoples lost records of their ancestry and traditions, leaving them cut off from their own past. Some knowledge survived in hidden or magically protected repositories, while oral accounts passed through the generations and became mingled with myth.

Among the surviving records are maps depicting the Devil's Hole in the northern Forelands. These predate the Cleansing, placing knowledge of the abyss among the few geographical records preserved from the ancient world.

The loss of knowledge is also believed to have caused a decline in magical and technical practices. Arts developed over millennia were forgotten, though the extent of that decline remains uncertain.
